The idea in brief

SoftServe learns how to reshape an update, not just its step size. It fits diagonal or Kronecker-factored matrices to observed changes in gradients. A soft constraint handles incompatible curvature observations; Newton–Schulz iterations make the matrix calculations GPU-friendly.

The experiments span a small RNN, an autoencoder, PINNs, and a 136M-parameter physics-informed diffusion model. The gains are substantial on several tasks—but do not carry over to matching Muon and SOAP on our language model.

01 / The soft secant idea

Learn curvature without
requiring an exact secant fit.

A single learning rate treats all parameter directions alike. When the loss is much steeper in some directions than others, this can make optimization slow. A quasi-Newton method instead maintains a matrix H that approximates inverse curvature and uses it to reshape the gradient.

x⁺ = x − ηHg

Here x is the parameter vector, g its gradient, η the learning rate, and H a positive-definite inverse-Hessian estimate. Momentum and update normalization enter the practical optimizer later.

What a secant pair tells us

We do not need to form a Hessian to learn about curvature. Take two parameter points. Let s = x_new − x_old be their displacement and y = g_new − g_old the change in gradient. Classical quasi-Newton methods ask the next estimate to satisfy the secant equation:

H_new y = s.

There is a catch. If H is positive definite and y is nonzero, then yᵀHy > 0. A perfect secant fit therefore requires sᵀy > 0. Non-convexity and stochastic gradients need not give us such a pair.

Replace the constraint with a penalty

The soft quasi-Newton objective of Berglund, Zhang, and Johansson [1] fits the secant equation without requiring equality:

One objective, two competing goals
H_new = argmin over H ≻ 0: ‖s − Hy‖²_H⁻¹ + D_ld(H_old, H) / λ

A small λ favors the previous estimate. A larger λ puts more emphasis on the new pair. Crucially, the exact solution stays positive definite even when the pair is incompatible with an exact secant fit.

What do the norm and divergence mean?

The weighted norm is ‖v‖²_H⁻¹ = vᵀH⁻¹v. For d-dimensional matrices, the log-determinant divergence is

D_ld = tr(H⁻¹H_old) − log det(H⁻¹H_old) − d.

It is nonnegative and vanishes when the matrices agree. It is also twice the KL divergence from N(0, H_old) to N(0, H). This links the update to a variational view of curvature fitting; related matrix equations also arise in Batch and Match [2].

The calculation behind the animation

We use s = (1, 0), y = (cos θ, sin θ), and H_old = I. Let V = I + λssᵀ and b = yᵀVy. The exact dense solution is

H_new = V − 4λ (Vy)(Vy)ᵀ / (1 + √(1 + 4λb))².

It satisfies λHyyᵀH + H = V. The displayed residual is ‖s − Hy‖₂ / ‖s‖₂. These browser calculations are independent of the paper’s experimental measurements.

03 / Diag and Kron

Put the structure
inside the objective.

The dense soft-QN update is still too large for a neural network. SoftServe optimizes the same objective within a structured family, rather than computing a dense update and compressing it afterward.

SoftServe-Diag

One positive value per parameter. The objective separates into scalar problems, each with a closed-form solution.

H = diag(h), hᵢ > 0

SoftServe-Kron

Two positive-definite factors per weight matrix. They capture interactions between rows and between columns.

H = L ⊗ R, Hg = vec(RGL)

For a weight matrix X ∈ ℝⁿˣᵐ, G is its matrix gradient, R is n × n, and L is m × m. The full inverse-curvature matrix would have (nm)² entries. Kron stores only n² + m².

Update one factor at a time

The two Kron factors are coupled, so we update them alternately. Each exact factor solve has a unique positive-definite solution and cannot increase the curvature-fitting objective. One sweep is not a joint global solve—and decreasing this fitting objective is not a guarantee that the training loss decreases.

04 / The training loop

Use the metric every step.
Refresh it occasionally.

1. Apply the current metric

H acts on a momentum direction m. We normalize the update in the learned metric, then take a step with learning rate η:

d = Hm / √(mᵀHm), x⁺ = x − ηd.

The update is zero when m is zero. With H = I, this gives our SoftServe-I control: the same normalized momentum update without a learned metric.

2. Form a secant pair every K updates

Curvature is refreshed every K parameter updates. In stochastic training, both endpoint gradients must use the same batch and randomness; otherwise their difference mixes parameter movement with a change in the objective.

Default K = 1010 training gradients + 1 replay
12345678910↶

Save the starting batch and gradient. After ten updates, replay that batch to form the secant pair and refresh the metric.

This adds about 10% more gradient evaluations, not necessarily 10% more runtime: factor updates also take time. For a deterministic objective, gradients already available from training can supply the endpoints.

3. Refresh the factors with matrix multiplications

Each factor solve reduces to a quadratic matrix equation:

QUQ + Q = V, U ⪰ 0, V ≻ 0.

Q is the factor being updated; U and V combine the secant pair with the current metric. The solution involves matrix square roots and inverses. Our Gram formulation exploits U = FFᵀ, and coupled Newton–Schulz iterations approximate the required matrix functions using matrix multiplications and additions.

Numerical solver settings

The experimental default uses 18 coupled root iterations and 10 inverse iterations. These are finite-precision approximations, not exact factor solves, so numerical residual and validity checks remain important. See the paper for the Gram formulation and iteration-count ablations.

05 / Experiments

From recurrent networks
to physics-informed diffusion.

We tune each method’s learning rate separately, then evaluate the selected configurations on three fresh seeds. The main comparison matches gradient evaluations, including secant replays, line searches, and loss-balancing gradients.

Memory across time. Compression across layers.

321 → 2.8M parameters

On Adding, Kron’s mean test MSE is 6.6 × 10⁻⁶, compared with 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ for Muon and 1.0 × 10⁻² for the identity-metric control. On MNIST, Kron reaches the lowest full-batch training objective and is close to Muon with minibatches.

Three PDEs, two sampling regimes

81,201 parameters

Kron achieves the lowest final training objective in five of six settings, including all three stochastic settings. Deterministic Wave is the exception: Adam and Adam + L-BFGS finish lower, with the combination best.

Larger physics-informed models

0.73M → 136M parameters

Kron is competitive with Muon on Allen–Cahn and has the lowest final mean loss on KdV. On the 136M-parameter U-Net for structural design, it also reaches a lower mean training objective than Adam, Muon, and SOAP at the matched budget.

Original paper figures, not new experiments. Lines are three-seed arithmetic means; bands span seed minimum–maximum. Failed cohorts retain individual traces. The paper’s appendix reports solution/test errors and wall-clock comparisons.

06 / Limitations

Useful curvature is
not a universal win.

A positive-definite estimate is a structural guarantee, not a promise of better optimization on every architecture. On our 16M-parameter GPT trained on 200M FineWeb tokens, Kron does not match Muon or SOAP.

AdamW4.786
SoftServe-Kron4.661
Muon4.440
SOAP4.416

Mean validation NLL, lower is better. These are the language-model results reported in the paper, not the physics-informed diffusion experiment.

λ matters, and a larger value is not automatically better. We keep it fixed within each reported run. Adaptive penalties, numerical accuracy, refresh frequency, and understanding when structured secants improve training remain important directions.

The takeaway is specific: structured quasi-Newton updates can be practical and effective for deep learning, with particularly encouraging results on the recurrent and physics-informed tasks we tested.

07 / Use SoftServe

Your model.
A learned update geometry.

The public PyTorch interface routes matrix weights to Kron and supports Adam, AdamW, or SoftServe-Diag for the remaining parameters. The README includes deterministic and stochastic training loops.

PyTorch · model-based API
from softserve import SoftServeKron

optimizer = SoftServeKron(
    model,
    lr=1e-3,
    lam=99,
    K=10,
    fallback="adamw",
    fallback_lr=3e-4,
)

An API example, not a universally tuned configuration. For stochastic training, use optimizer.step(closure) so both secant endpoints share the batch and randomness.
